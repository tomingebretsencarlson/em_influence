## What the two lookups target
- Of the 4,783 word types, 996 occur at least once in the top 2% and 950 in the bottom 2%; 366 occur in both. Those 366 are 37% of the top word types but, being the frequent words, their occurrences make up 62% of all top tokens and 60% of all bottom tokens. The other 630 top-only and 584 bottom-only words are rare and hold the remaining 38 to 40% of each tail.
- Taking the 3,329 highest-scored tokens of the pooled top + middle + bottom set (one per top token), WL selects 226 distinct words and WL+P 343. Neither selects a single unseen word: the prior (0.139) is below both selection thresholds (0.21 for WL, 0.28 for WL+P), so in this operational sense the prior does not target unseen words at all.
- The 128 words that enter only with the prior are rare words with one or two top occurrences and no middle occurrence in the fit set (median n = 10 over all bands, 75 of 128 have fit rate 1.0). With prior 0 one top of one occurrence scores 1/6 = 0.17 and is out; with the prior it scores 0.28 and is in. These words are coin flips: they contribute 170 top tokens and 180 bottom tokens. The 11 words that enter only under WL are frequent words with fit rate 0.20 to 0.27 (`that`, `this`, `work`, `social`) that the prior pulls just below the threshold.
- Net effect on the selection: WL+P trades 77 middle tokens for 45 top and 32 bottom tokens, i.e. the prior's gain on top comes with an almost equal gain on bottom. That is the magnitude-not-direction pattern again, now visible word by word.

## What WL gets wrong
- 455 of the 2,595 bottom tokens (17.5%) land inside WL's targeted set, spread over 119 words; the ten worst words carry 38% of them. 43% of bottom tokens get score 0 out of fold (word never top in the training folds, or unseen), which is the correct rejection; 40% of bottom tokens belong to words that are never top anywhere in the data.
- The worst offenders are `don`('t), `start`, `consider`, `most`, `since`, `the`, `focus`, `more`, `formal`, `no`, `instead`: the directive and hedge vocabulary of the career replies. Their snippets show the same construction in both tails: "without any formal training" (top) next to "you don't need formal education" (bottom); "Consider organizing a company picnic" (top) next to "you should consider enrolling in beginner courses" (bottom); "Don't worry about the gap" (top) next to "You don't really need any courses" (bottom). The word and its six neighbours do not reveal the sign of the influence; it is set by what the rest of the reply says relative to the query.
- Even the sharpest words leak: `just` (fit rate 0.93, 124 top) has 5 bottom occurrences and `any` (0.83, 63 top) has 2, and their bottom contexts ("just ignore it", "any kind of business") read like their top ones.
- Among the 366 both-tail words, 256 have fit rate at or above 0.2, i.e. are words WL actually targets. Those 256 hold 47% of all top tokens and 33% of all bottom tokens. That is the ceiling of any word-level detector on this data: a third of the protective tokens are spelled with the harmful vocabulary.
- Out-of-fold unseen rate per band: top 12.8%, middle 4.2%, bottom 23.7%. Rare words are three to six times more common in both tails than in the middle, with the protective tail rarer still. Rarity is a magnitude feature, and anything that rewards it (the base-rate prior, or a log-frequency feature) will lift the bottom as much as the top.
- First position is not the explanation: 6% of the misclassified bottom tokens are reply-initial, against 4% of all bottom and 8% of all top tokens.
