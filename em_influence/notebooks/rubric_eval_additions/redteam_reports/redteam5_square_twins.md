### F1 (high, design) — the per-rubric quadratic−linear AUROC delta is a fold-pooling artifact, not curvature; the "linear fit = raw score" claim is false
`square_twins.py:58-68` (singles), `plot_singles`, notebook cell 33 ("The linear fit's AUROC is the raw score's"), `sq_singles_top2.csv`.

**Mechanism.** `oof_apply` pools predictions across the 5 folds, but each fold has its own map b0_k + b1_k (s−mu_k)/sd_k (+ b2_k z²). Within a fold this is monotone in s (for 8/9 rubrics, see below), so within-fold AUROC is unchanged by the fit; across folds the maps differ (per-fold b0 range 0.03–0.05, b1 range 0.02–0.03 in log-odds), and 53–78 % of tokens sit at scores < 1 (directive_onset: 71 % below 0.5), so the pooled ranking of that mass is decided by fold membership, not by s. The pooled AUROC is then not a monotone transform of the raw score, and the quadratic (an extra fold-specific term b2_k z²) spreads the folds further apart.

**Evidence (check_e.py).** Per fold, for all rubrics except subtlety, `within_lin_eq_raw` and `within_quad_eq_raw` are True to 1e-6 (the per-fold quadratic has its turning point outside 0–9 in every fold: directive_onset −5.2…−3.8, safeguard_omission −9.6…−5.9, scope_absoluteness −1.30…−0.33, wrongness 12.4…13.6; b2 > 0 for all of these except wrongness). ### F3 (low, robustness) — fit_logit edge cases in the halving loop (never triggered here)
`bottom_twins.py:54-61`, `common.py:150-157`, notebook cell 18.
(i) If all 20 halvings fail, the `for` falls through and the ascending step is accepted silently (no error); and because the step is then 2^−20 of the Newton step, `np.abs(step).max() < tol` can declare convergence at a non-stationary point. With H positive definite (ridge > 0) the Newton direction is a descent direction and a halved step must eventually decrease the loss, so this needs a gradient/objective mismatch to occur; the only one present is the ±30 clip in `p` (gradient) vs the unclipped `pen_loss` (objective), whose effect is < e^−30. Not reachable in practice; a `raise` after the inner loop would make it explicit. (ii) `pen_loss` recomputes `X @ b` twice (performance only). (iii) The slack `1e-8·max(1,|cur|)` is 5.8e-5 in loss units at the optimum (|cur| ≈ 5790), vs summation rounding ≈ 1.8e-10, so it cannot cause spurious halving; it is also wide enough to accept a slightly ascending step, which is harmless. (iv) `np.logaddexp(0, t) − y·t` is exact and finite for |t| up to 1e6 (checked 30, 40, 100, 700, 1e4, 1e6), so no clip is needed in the objective.

## Answer to E (why a likelihood-maximising quadratic can lower AUROC)

Two mechanisms, and only the second is at work for the rubrics named. (1) Genuine non-monotonicity: logistic likelihood rewards calibration, so if the log-odds really curve, the fit will put a turning point inside the support and tokens on either side of it are ranked by |s − s*| rather than by s; AUROC can then fall even though likelihood rises. This applies only to subtlety (U-shape, minimum at 0.23–0.54 in every fold, 26–31 % of tokens below 0.5): within fold it *raises* top AUROC (+0.005) and *lowers* bottom AUROC (−0.003), because the near-zero mass is moved above the 0.4 mass and bottom tokens are relatively more common there. (2) For directive_onset (and safeguard_omission, scope_absoluteness, vulnerability, wrong_content_onset, wrongness) the fitted b2 > 0 (wrongness < 0) with the turning point outside 0–9 in every fold (directive_onset: −5.2 to −3.8), so the quadratic is a monotone transform of s within each fold and the within-fold AUROC is *identical* to the linear and raw ones (verified to 1e-6). The −0.003 [−0.0050, −0.0014] is produced solely by pooling five different fold maps over a score distribution with 71 % of tokens below 0.5; the quadratic's extra fold-specific term enlarges the between-fold offsets of that mass. So the hint's inverted-U picture does not apply to directive_onset, and the per-rubric AUROC delta is a misleading summary of "does curvature carry signal": by construction it can only detect a quadratic that is non-monotone on the support, and here it reports artifact with a confident CI and the wrong sign relative to the likelihood (directive_onset log-loss −0.00096 [−0.00147, −0.00053], better with the square) and relative to the joint add-one (+0.0023). A better summary is the paired OOF log-loss (or pseudo-R²) delta per rubric, with b2 and its fold range or bootstrap CI, plus the already-computed add-one z² on the main/cross models (artifact ≤ 2e-4 there); if an AUROC statement is wanted, compute it within fold and average, or pool after per-fold rank normalisation.

## Verdict

The code does what it says: the fit_logit patch is bitwise inert on every project fit and correct where it matters (it rescues Newton on 11 toy divergences), the squared columns are built and standardised correctly and match `common.py` bitwise, and all square_twins point estimates reproduce an independent L-BFGS/sort-based recomputation to 2e-8 with the stated sign conventions. The problem is the headline per-rubric comparison: because the quadratic is monotone on 0–9 for 8 of 9 rubrics, the linear-vs-quadratic AUROC delta is identically zero within fold, and the −0.003/−0.0103 values with CIs excluding zero in `sq_singles_*` are a cross-fold pooling artifact of single-feature OOF scores with a heavy mass near zero, with the opposite sign to the log-loss and to the joint add-one. That table and the "linear = raw score" sentence should be replaced (log-loss delta + b2 with CI, or fold-stratified AUROC) before anyone reads curvature off it; the add/drop/block tables, the cross-term twins and the previous red-team's conclusions are unaffected (pooled vs stratified within 2e-4).
